At a glance

The short answers

What is Thin Air?

An 11-day small-group expedition across Tibet, from Chengdu through Lhasa, Gyantse, Shigatse and Sakya to the North Face of Everest, finishing in Kathmandu.

Who is it for?

Curious travellers who want Tibetan monasteries, high passes and Everest without trekking, in a group of no more than 16 guests.

What are the highlights?

The Potala Palace and Jokhang, the Sera debate, Yamdrok Tso, the Karo La glacier, Tashilhunpo, a night below the North Face of Everest and the train back to Lhasa.

How hard is the altitude?

Lhasa sits at 3,656 m and the route crosses passes above 5,000 m. The pace is gradual, vehicles carry oxygen, and only one night is near 5,000 m. No trekking is required.

The altitude ledger

Metres above sea level · day by day

Travel information

Before you go
Visas & permits

You enter China at Chengdu on a Chinese visa, or visa-free if your nationality qualifies. Check the rules for your passport before booking. Your passport needs at least six months' validity and two blank pages.

Foreign visitors can only enter Tibet on an organised tour with a licensed guide. We arrange the Tibet Travel Permit and the Everest region permits; we need passport and visa copies about 30 days before departure. Permits are occasionally suspended at short notice, and we will advise if that happens.

Nepal issues visas on arrival at Kathmandu airport for most nationalities.

Altitude & health

Lhasa sits at 3,656 m and the route crosses passes above 5,000 m. The itinerary climbs gradually, but everyone feels the altitude. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol for the first two days and walk slowly.

Speak to your doctor about altitude medication before you travel. Guests with heart or lung conditions need medical clearance. Insurance must cover medical evacuation from high altitude.

Fitness & pace

No trekking is required. Expect stairs at the Potala Palace, monastery walks of one to two hours and long road days of five to seven hours with regular stops. A moderate level of fitness is enough.

Weather in September

September is the end of the monsoon and the start of the clear season. Lhasa days are mild and sunny, often around 18–20 °C, and nights cool to single figures. At Everest, nights fall below freezing and wind is common. Skies are usually at their clearest in the mornings.

What to pack

Layers: thermal base, fleece, down jacket, windproof shell, warm hat and gloves. Sunglasses, SPF 50 sunscreen and lip balm are essential at altitude. Comfortable walking shoes, a refillable bottle and a small daypack. Modest dress for monasteries.

Money, connectivity & photography

The currency is the Chinese yuan (CNY). Foreign cards are accepted in some hotels but rarely elsewhere, so carry cash. Mobile data works in towns; many international apps are blocked in China. Photography is restricted inside many monasteries, and some charge camera fees. Always ask before photographing people.

Flights

Fly into Chengdu (CTU or TFU) by 14 September, or 12 September with the panda extension. The expedition ends in Kathmandu (KTM) on 24 September, or 27 September with the post-extension. For the Lhasa to Kathmandu flight, ask for a window on the left side for views of the Himalaya.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
Do I need a permit to visit Tibet?

Yes. Foreign visitors need a Tibet Travel Permit and must travel with a licensed guide; Everest needs additional permits. We arrange them from passport and visa copies about 30 days before departure.

How high does Thin Air go?

The highest point is Gyawu La at about 5,198 m, and the Everest night is at about 5,000 m. Most nights are between 3,656 m and 4,280 m.

Will I see Everest?

September usually brings clear mornings after the monsoon, and the route spends a sunset and a sunrise below the North Face. Mountain weather can still hide the summit.

Is there trekking?

No. Expect stairs at the Potala Palace, monastery walks of one to two hours and long road days with regular stops. A moderate level of fitness is enough.

How do I get to Chengdu and home from Kathmandu?

Fly into Chengdu Shuangliu (CTU) and home from Kathmandu (KTM). The Chengdu–Lhasa and Lhasa–Kathmandu flights are part of the expedition routing.

When is the best time to visit Tibet?

Late September and October bring clear skies after the monsoon and comfortable days, which is why Thin Air departs in mid-September.
