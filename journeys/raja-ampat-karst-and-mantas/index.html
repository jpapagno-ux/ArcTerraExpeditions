At a glance

The short answers

What is Karst & Mantas?

An 11-day, 10-night private voyage through Raja Ampat in West Papua, Indonesia, aboard the 50-metre phinisi Silolona, round trip from Sorong.

Who is it for?

Divers and snorkellers travelling together, families with confident swimmers, photographers, and anyone who wants mantas and limestone karst without a crowded liveaboard.

What are the highlights?

Mantas at Manta Sandy, Blue Magic and Magic Mountain; Cape Kri's record fish count; the Piaynemo viewpoint; crossing the equator to Wayag; Balbulol lagoon and the Boo Windows in Misool.

How active is it?

Easy to moderate, with two optional climbs (Piaynemo and the Wayag peak) and up to three dives or snorkels a day. Two overnight passages in open sea.

The route

Sorong · Yenbuba Bay · Cape Kri · Arborek · Manta Sandy · Blue Magic · Piaynemo · Wayag · Fam Islands · Magic Mountain · Balbulol · Boo Windows · Fiabacet · Daram

DAY 01 / 11AX·021 · D01

Sorong — Yenbuba Bay

Board Silolona in Sorong, sail west into the Dampier Strait and slip into the water under the Yenbuba jetty before the first sunset at anchor.

The crew meets you at Sorong airport and takes you to the harbour, where Silolona waits under her dark sails. Lunch is served as she clears the port and heads west for the Dampier Strait. In the afternoon the snorkellers drift under the Yenbuba jetty among schooling fish while the divers make an easy check dive below them. Sundowners on deck, then dinner at anchor off Mansuar.

DAY 02 / 11AX·021 · D02

Cape Kri — Arborek

Cape Kri, where a single dive once logged a record 374 species of fish, then the village of Arborek and its jetty reef.

Cape Kri is the dive that made Raja Ampat's name: a current-swept point where a scientist once counted 374 species of fish on a single dive. Divers drop onto the point; snorkellers drift the sheltered shallows with turtles and reef sharks. In the afternoon Silolona moves to Arborek, a small village known for its craftwork and community-run reef, for a walk ashore and a sunset swim off the jetty.

DAY 03 / 11AX·021 · D03

Mantas of the Dampier Strait

Manta Sandy at first light, where reef mantas circle the cleaning stations close enough to watch from the surface, then Blue Magic for the divers.

The first of the two manta days. At Manta Sandy, a sandy cleaning station off Arborek, reef mantas come in to be cleaned by small wrasse, circling slowly over the rubble. Divers settle on the sand; snorkellers float above, and the mantas often rise to the surface. The afternoon is for the divers at Blue Magic, a submerged pinnacle with oceanic mantas and big schools, while the snorkellers kayak the Mioskon shore.

DAY 04 / 11AX·021 · D04

Piaynemo

West along the strait to the karst of Piaynemo: the stairway to the view everyone comes for, then the lagoons and reefs below it.

Silolona sails west along the Dampier Strait to the Fam group and anchors off Piaynemo. The wooden stairway climbs a little over three hundred steps to a lookout over the classic Raja Ampat picture: dozens of forested limestone islets in a turquoise lagoon. Go early, before the day boats.

Afterwards, kayak the channels between the karst, snorkel the hard-coral gardens or dive the outer walls.

DAY 05 / 11AX·021 · D05

Across the Equator to Wayag

North under sail across the equator to Wayag, the karst archipelago at the top of Raja Ampat, and the scramble to its summit for sunset.

The longest sail of the northern days. With a fair wind the crew sets Silolona's sails and she crosses the equator heading for Wayag, at about ten minutes north. Expect a line-crossing toast on deck.

Wayag is the outer edge of Raja Ampat: steep, jungle-topped limestone islands around lagoons so clear the blacktip reef sharks show from the tender. In the late afternoon, scramble up the peak for the view over the whole archipelago as the sun drops.

DAY 06 / 11AX·021 · D06

Wayag

A whole day in the Wayag lagoons: blacktip reef sharks in the shallows, kayaks through the karst, and nobody else in sight.

A day with no passage at all. The tenders explore the lagoons, where young blacktip reef sharks patrol the sand in knee-deep water, and the kayaks go where the tenders can't. Divers can take the outer walls; everyone else swims, paddles, naps and swims again.

Wayag sits inside a marine protected area patrolled by local rangers, and the visit fee goes to them. This is the quiet heart of the voyage.

DAY 07 / 11AX·021 · D07

Fam Islands, then South

South to the hard-coral gardens of the Fam Islands, then the long night passage across the Seram Sea to Misool.

Back south through the morning to the Fam Islands for some of the healthiest hard coral in Raja Ampat, at sites such as Melissa's Garden: tables, plates and staghorn in shallow, sunlit water that suits snorkellers as well as divers.

After dinner Silolona starts the longest passage of the voyage, about ninety nautical miles south across the open Seram Sea to Misool. Most guests sleep through it; the crew does not.

DAY 08 / 11AX·021 · D08

Magic Mountain — Balbulol

The second manta day at Magic Mountain in Misool, then an afternoon paddling the karst lagoon of Balbulol.

Misool is the southern half of the story: older, wilder and protected by one of the largest no-take marine reserves in Indonesia. Magic Mountain is a submerged seamount with cleaning stations where oceanic and reef mantas come in from the blue, with schools of jacks and barracuda over the top. Divers go down to the stations; snorkellers ride the tender to the sheltered edge and watch for mantas passing below.

In the afternoon Silolona moves to Balbulol, a maze of karst towers and lagoons where the kayaks go through narrow channels into still water the colour of glass.

DAY 09 / 11AX·021 · D09

Boo Windows — Fiabacet

Through the two windows of Boo Rock, then the soft-coral pinnacles of Fiabacet, with a night dive for those who want one.

Boo Rock breaks the surface off southern Misool, and just below the waterline two holes have worn right through it: the Boo Windows. Divers swim through; snorkellers look down through them from above. The reef around is thick with soft coral, fans and fish.

Fiabacet's pinnacles take the afternoon, and the divers can add a night dive after dinner.

Silolona

A 50-metre phinisi built in 2004 by the Konjo shipwrights of South Sulawesi, Silolona was one of the first traditional Indonesian sailing ships fitted out for private charter, and she still sails under her dark gaff-rigged sails. Five suites, a crew of about seventeen and a full PADI dive centre with three tenders. She is chartered whole, to one group.

Travel information

Before you go
Getting there

Fly to Sorong (SOQ) in West Papua, usually through Jakarta (CGK) or Makassar (UPG). Arrive the day before: flights to Papua run late more often than ships do, and Silolona sails on the day. The crew meets you at Sorong airport on day one.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities can buy an Indonesian visa on arrival or apply online for an e-VOA before travel. Passports need at least six months' validity. Check the current rules for your nationality before you book.

Raja Ampat permit

Every visitor buys a Raja Ampat marine park entry permit (the PIN card), which funds the reserve and its rangers. Wayag and some Misool sites charge a small local fee on top. The crew arranges both. [Current fee to confirm.]

Health & insurance

Speak to a travel clinic about malaria and vaccinations for West Papua. Medical facilities are limited and far apart, so travel insurance must include dive cover and medical evacuation.

Weather & sea

Silolona sails Raja Ampat from about January to April and September to November. Expect 27–32 °C on deck and 28–30 °C in the water. The long passages to Wayag and Misool are in open sea; the crew times them for the calmest conditions.

What to pack

Light clothes, a hat, reef-safe sunscreen, a rash guard or 3 mm wetsuit, grippy shoes for Piaynemo and Wayag, a dry bag, any personal dive kit and your certification card. Soft bags only.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
What is the Karst & Mantas voyage?

Karst & Mantas is an ARCX private charter aboard the phinisi Silolona: 11 days and 10 nights, Sorong round trip, through Raja Ampat in West Papua. It covers the Dampier Strait, Piaynemo, Wayag across the equator and the Misool karst, with two days built around mantas.

Can snorkellers and divers travel together?

Yes. Every stop on this route has a way in from the surface: mantas often rise to the surface at Manta Sandy, the Fam reefs are shallow, and the Boo Windows can be seen from above. On the few dives that suit divers only, such as Blue Magic, snorkellers kayak or explore by tender.

When is the best time to see manta rays in Raja Ampat?

Mantas are seen all year, but the cleaning stations of the Dampier Strait and Misool are usually busiest from about October to April. This voyage is proposed for November 2027, with manta days at Manta Sandy and Blue Magic (day 3) and Magic Mountain (day 8).

How do I get to Sorong?

Fly to Sorong (SOQ) in West Papua, usually through Jakarta or Makassar. Arrive the day before, as Silolona sails on day one. The crew meets you at the airport and takes you to the harbour.

How many guests does Silolona take?

Silolona has five suites and is chartered whole, to one group of up to about ten guests (twelve with children), with a crew of about seventeen and a PADI dive centre on board.

How much does the charter cost?

Silolona is listed from US$18,700 a night plus 12% Indonesian VAT, so ten nights start at about US$187,000 plus VAT for the whole yacht, or about US$20,900 a guest for ten. Flights and the Raja Ampat permit are extra.
