The short answers

What is the Classic Antarctica Air-Cruise?

Antarctica21’s most popular expedition: eight days and seven nights from Punta Arenas, Chile, with a two-hour flight to King George Island and five days aboard the expedition ship Magellan Explorer in the South Shetland Islands and on the Antarctic Peninsula.

Why fly instead of sail the Drake?

The flight replaces two days each way on the Drake Passage, among the roughest seas on Earth, so more of the trip is spent in Antarctica and less in transit. A weather window decides the flight time.

What will I see?

Penguin colonies (chinstrap, gentoo), seals, humpback and minke whales in summer, glacier fronts, sculpted icebergs, a continental landing and the Lemaire Channel. Landing sites change daily with ice and weather.

How active is it?

Easy to moderate. Twice-daily Zodiac outings with short walks on snow and rock; boots are provided. Kayaking and snowshoeing are optional extras on some departures.

Punta Arenas · the Strait of Magellan

DAY 02 / 08 · TUE 5 JAN 2027PC·A21 · D02

Fly south · King George Island

A two-hour flight from Punta Arenas over the Drake Passage to King George Island. Walk past Chile’s Frei station to the shore, then step into a Zodiac for the ride out to Magellan Explorer.

The flight leaves Punta Arenas in the morning, weather permitting, and crosses the Drake Passage in about two hours instead of two days at sea. On the gravel runway of Fildes Peninsula you meet the expedition team, walk through Chile’s Frei station and Russia’s Bellingshausen, then take the first Zodiac ride of the voyage out to the ship. Safety drills, a Zodiac briefing and dinner as Magellan Explorer heads into the Bransfield Strait.

DAY 03 / 08 · WED 6 JAN 2027PC·A21 · D03

South Shetlands · chinstraps & a volcano

Morning landing at Half Moon Island among chinstrap penguins below the Livingston Island glaciers, then the volcanic black-sand shore of Baily Head on Deception Island.

Half Moon Island is a crescent of rock with a busy chinstrap penguin colony and fur seals hauled out on the beach, all framed by the ice cliffs of Livingston Island. After lunch the ship moves to Deception Island, a flooded volcanic caldera, for a Zodiac landing at Baily Head when the surf allows, with one of the larger chinstrap colonies in the region. Overnight the ship crosses the Bransfield Strait towards the Antarctic Peninsula.

DAY 04 / 08 · THU 7 JAN 2027PC·A21 · D04

Cuverville & the continent

Wake among grounded icebergs at Cuverville Island, home to one of the largest gentoo colonies on the Peninsula, then set foot on the Antarctic continent at Neko Harbour.

The channel around Cuverville Island catches icebergs drifting out of the Errera Channel, and the Zodiac cruise weaves between sculpted blue bergs before a landing at the gentoo colony. In the afternoon the ship enters Andvord Bay for Neko Harbour, a continental landing below an active glacier front: listen for calving, climb the snow slope for the view and watch for leopard seals hunting along the ice edge. A quiet night at anchor in the bay.

DAY 05 / 08 · FRI 8 JAN 2027PC·A21 · D05

Paradise, the Lemaire & iceberg alley

A Zodiac cruise below the glacier fronts of Paradise Harbour, the narrow Lemaire Channel between sheer peaks, and an afternoon among stranded giants in iceberg alley off Pléneau Island.

Paradise Harbour is ringed by glaciers that reach the water; the morning is a slow Zodiac cruise past the Argentine Brown station and ice cliffs, with a landing for the view when conditions allow. Ice permitting, the ship then threads the Lemaire Channel, 11 km long and as little as 1.6 km wide, between walls of rock and hanging glaciers. At its southern end, the shallows off Pléneau Island catch icebergs that run aground and slowly carve into arches and towers: the best Zodiac cruising of the voyage.

DAY 06 / 08 · SAT 9 JAN 2027PC·A21 · D06

Port Lockroy & the whales of the Gerlache

Port Lockroy, the restored British base with its museum and post office, then an afternoon in the Gerlache Strait with feeding humpback whales before the run north.

Port Lockroy on Goudier Island was a British research base, now a museum and the southernmost working post office you are likely to visit, surrounded by nesting gentoos; send a postcard home (landing subject to the station’s operating season). Nearby Jougla Point has whale bones and blue-eyed shags. In the afternoon the ship slows in the Gerlache Strait, where humpback and minke whales feed in summer, and Zodiacs go out if the whales are close. Farewell dinner, then the overnight run north to King George Island.

Travel information

Before you go
Getting there

Fly to Punta Arenas (PUQ) in southern Chile, usually via Santiago (SCL). Arrive by mid-afternoon on day 1 for the mandatory briefing at the Explorers House. On day 8, book flights leaving after about 11:00.

The Antarctic flight

The two-hour charter flight to King George Island leaves when the weather on the Fildes Peninsula allows. The team confirms the window the evening before; if the flight is delayed, the hotel and plans in Punta Arenas are taken care of.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities enter Chile visa-free for tourism; check the rules for your passport. Antarctica itself needs no visa, but all visits follow the Antarctic Treaty and IAATO visitor guidelines.

What to wear

Layers: thermal base, fleece, a waterproof outer shell and waterproof trousers, warm hat, gloves and sunglasses. Boots for the Zodiac landings are lent on board. Temperatures are usually −2 to +3 °C in summer; wind makes it feel colder.

Health & fitness

You need to step in and out of Zodiacs with help and walk on snow and uneven rock. There is a doctor on board; travel insurance must include medical evacuation from Antarctica.

Biosecurity

Clothing, bags and boots are vacuumed and disinfected before landings so no seeds or soil reach Antarctica. No food is taken ashore and you keep 5 m from wildlife.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
How long is the flight to Antarctica?

About two hours from Punta Arenas, Chile, to King George Island in the South Shetland Islands. It replaces the two-day crossing of the Drake Passage by sea in each direction.

Which ship is used on the Classic Antarctica Air-Cruise?

Magellan Explorer, an expedition ship built in 2019 for Antarctica21’s air-cruises, carrying up to 88 guests with 10 Zodiacs. Some departures use Antarctica21’s Magellan Discoverer.

Do I actually set foot on the Antarctic continent?

Usually yes. The route aims for a continental landing such as Neko Harbour on the Antarctic Peninsula, as well as island landings in the South Shetlands. Ice and weather decide each day.

When is the best time to go?

The season runs from late November to early March. December and January have the longest days and penguin chicks; February and March bring the most whale sightings.

What happens if the flight is delayed by weather?

The flight waits for a safe weather window on King George Island. Antarctica21 builds a contingency plan into the itinerary, with the hotel and activities in Punta Arenas or extra time aboard covered.

How much does the Classic Antarctica Air-Cruise cost?

Antarctica21 publishes fares from US$13,795 per person for the 8-day Classic Antarctica Air-Cruise, with offers of up to US$4,000 per person on select 2027–28 departures.
