Seven nights aboard the phinisi Dunia Baru through the Banda Islands, the volcanic archipelago where nutmeg once decided the fate of empires. Run and its Manhattan story, the lava-flow corals and the 666 m cone of Gunung Api, the pinnacles of Batu Kapal, Fort Belgica, Pulau Ai's cinnamon tea, a day under full sail and the pelagic walls of Nusa Laut.

At a glance

The short answers

What is Nutmeg & Fire?

An eight-day, seven-night private voyage through Indonesia's Banda Islands aboard the phinisi Dunia Baru, round trip from Ambon in Maluku.

Who is it for?

Divers, snorkellers and travellers who want history ashore: the Dutch spice forts, nutmeg plantations and village life of the Spice Islands.

What are the highlights?

Run Island and the Manhattan trade, the Banda Neira lava-flow corals, Gunung Api's summit, the Batu Kapal pinnacles, the Mandarin fish at dusk and a day under sail.

How active is it?

Easy to moderate. One optional steep volcano climb, gentle village walks and up to three dives or snorkels a day. Every activity is optional.

DAY 01 / 08AX·014 · D01

Ambon — Molana

Board Dunia Baru in Ambon and cruise to Molana for an afternoon on the beach and reef. At sunset the anchor comes up for the night passage south to the Spice Islands.

Your journey begins as you embark on Dunia Baru in Ambon, Maluku. Once the crew has welcomed you aboard your floating home and you have settled in, enjoy breakfast as we cruise to the nearby island of Molana. Spend the afternoon as you please: a nap on the beach, a leisurely snorkel over the surrounding reefs, or drifting in the warm shallows. As the sun sets on this first day, we cast off towards the fabled Spice Islands.

DAY 02 / 08AX·014 · D02

Nailaka — Run

Wake to the sandbank of Nailaka beside Run, the nutmeg island the British traded for Manhattan. Dive the wall for hammerheads, then cruise into Banda Neira at dusk.

Wake with the sun as we approach the slivers of sand around Nailaka, in the shadow of the history-altering island of Run. This nutmeg-covered island carries one of history's great footnotes: here the Dutch struck a deal with the British, exchanging Run for a small piece of land on the eastern seaboard of North America. That land was New Amsterdam, now Manhattan. After lounging on the sandbank, descend along a dramatic wall for the chance of hammerhead sharks rising from the deep. Cocktails and canapés on deck, dinner under the stars, and we arrive into the safe harbour of Banda Neira as evening settles in.

DAY 03 / 08AX·014 · D03

Banda Neira

Dive the coral of the north lava flow, climb the 666 m Gunung Api volcano, then the pinnacles of Batu Kapal. Afternoon in Banda Neira: Fort Belgica, spice shops and a nutmeg plantation.

After breakfast we start with freediving or scuba around the north lava flow, home to some of the most spectacular hard coral you are ever likely to see. Those feeling adventurous can hike to the summit of the 666-metre Gunung Api volcano, where the whole Banda archipelago unfolds beneath you. For the second dive we visit arguably the standout site of the journey, Batu Kapal, where pinnacles rise from the depths of the Banda Sea, teeming with life at every level. In the afternoon, stroll the lanes of Banda Neira: pick up local spices, explore Fort Belgica and wander through a working nutmeg plantation.

DAY 04 / 08AX·014 · D04

Gunung Api — Banda Besar

A morning return to Batu Kapal, then motorbikes through the nutmeg and clove plantations of Banda Besar, a village welcome, and a twilight dive for the Mandarin fish.

Breakfast in the shadow of the volcano's cone before another morning dive at Batu Kapal, a site that never disappoints twice. After lunch on board we make for Banda Besar, the largest of the Banda Islands, to explore by motorbike through nutmeg and clove plantations, past historic ruins and into a local community where children sometimes perform traditional dances for visitors. We return to Dunia Baru just before sunset for a twilight dive with a very special guest: the elusive Mandarin fish.

DAY 05 / 08AX·014 · D05

Pulau Ai

Walk the spice plantations of Pulau Ai to Fort Revenge, take cinnamon tea and nutmeg jam on a colonial balcony, snorkel sheltered reefs, and return to Banda Neira as the volcano glows.

We sail to Pulau Ai, where history and nature meet at every turn. Ashore, we walk through the famed spice plantations to Fort Revenge, a name that tells its own story of colonial ambition and resistance. Rusted cannons lie in the streets as locals go about their morning, and we end the walk on the balcony of a seaside colonial building, sipping cinnamon tea and tasting local snacks with nutmeg jam. In the afternoon, a cooling snorkel over sheltered reefs before we return towards Banda Neira as the volcano glows in the late light.

DAY 06 / 08AX·014 · D06

Banda Sea

A last dive at Batu Kapal and a snorkel at Pisang, then the sails go up and Dunia Baru crosses the open Banda Sea. Take the tender out to see her under full canvas.

After one final dive at Batu Kapal and a snorkel on Pisang's reefs, the crew raises the sails and Dunia Baru comes alive in her full glory. Feel the wind fill the canvas as we cross the Banda Sea, one of the most remote stretches of ocean in the world. Hop into the tender and drift away from the ship to see Dunia Baru under sail, her silhouette framed by volcanic islands and open sky. The afternoon is unhurried: read on deck, swim off the stern, or let the rhythm of the sea do its work.

Nusa Laut — Molana

Snorkel the north wall of Nusa Laut for dogtooth tuna and, with luck, a dugong. A last afternoon on Molana and a farewell feast on board.

Travel information

Before you go
Getting there

Fly to Ambon's Pattimura Airport (AMQ), usually via Jakarta (CGK) or Makassar (UPG). Arrive the day before or on an early flight on day one; the crew meets you at the airport. On day eight, book flights leaving after 12:00.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities can buy an Indonesian visa on arrival or apply online for an e-VOA before travel. Passports need at least six months' validity and blank pages. Check the current rules for your nationality before booking.

Diving & snorkelling

Bring your certification card and logbook. Some sites, such as Batu Kapal and the walls near Run, can have currents and are best for experienced divers; every site has a snorkel option from the tender. Allow 24 hours between your last dive and your flight home.

Health

Speak to a travel clinic about vaccinations and malaria prevention for Maluku. The nearest hospital with full facilities is in Ambon; travel insurance must include medical evacuation and diving.

Weather & sea

Warm year-round, around 26–31 °C, with water around 27–29 °C. The open-water crossings (Molana to Run, and Banda to Nusa Laut) are overnight or under sail and can have swell; bring a seasickness remedy if you need one.

What to pack

Light clothes, a sarong or long trousers and covered shoulders for village and fort visits, sturdy shoes for Gunung Api, a hat, reef-safe sunscreen, a light rash guard, a dry bag and a headtorch. Soft bags store best on board.

Money & connectivity

Carry Indonesian rupiah in small notes for village purchases and spices; cards are rarely accepted in the Bandas. Mobile signal is patchy away from Ambon and Banda Neira.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
Where do the Banda Islands lie?

The Banda Islands are a small volcanic group in the Banda Sea, in Indonesia's Maluku province, about 115 nautical miles south-east of Ambon. This voyage starts and ends in Ambon, the provincial capital.

How do I get to Ambon?

Ambon's Pattimura Airport (AMQ) has daily flights from Jakarta and Makassar. Most guests fly in the night before or on an early flight on day one. Book departures after midday on the last day.

Do I need to scuba dive?

No. Every dive site on this route has a snorkelling option from the tender, and the land days (Gunung Api, Banda Neira, Banda Besar, Pulau Ai) need no diving at all. Certified divers can dive up to three times a day.

Will I see hammerhead sharks?

Hammerheads are seen along the walls near Run and in the outer Banda Sea in some seasons, but they are wild animals and never guaranteed. The crew will plan dives for the best chance on the day.

Why is Run Island famous?

In the 1667 Treaty of Breda the English gave up their claim to Run, a nutmeg island, and the Dutch recognised English control of New Amsterdam, which became New York. The voyage anchors at the Nailaka sandbank beside Run on day two.

How hard is the Gunung Api climb?

Gunung Api is 666 metres high. The path is steep and loose near the top and takes most people one and a half to two hours up. It is optional and starts early to avoid the heat.

When is the best time to sail the Banda Sea?

The calmer transition seasons, roughly March to April and October to November, are the usual windows for Banda voyages. Exact Dunia Baru departure dates are to be confirmed.
