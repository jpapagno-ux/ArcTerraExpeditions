The short answers

What is The Long Left?

A sample eight-day ARCX Surf expedition by private surf charter from Padang, West Sumatra, through the Mentawai Islands, following the best swell, wind and tide each day.

Who is it for?

Intermediate to advanced surfers comfortable on shallow reef, for up to ten guests. Partners can snorkel, fish, paddle and explore the villages.

Where will I surf?

Reef breaks around the Playgrounds islands, south Siberut, north Sipora, Katiet in south Sipora and the west coast of Pagai Utara. Exact breaks are chosen daily by the guide [break names TBC].

When is the season?

The main swell season runs from about April to October, when long-period swells from the Southern Ocean arrive and morning winds are often offshore.

DAY 01 / 08AXS·01 · D01

Padang — the crossing

Fly into Padang, board the surf charter in the harbour and rack your boards. A safety and reef briefing at sunset, then the overnight crossing of about 80 nautical miles to the Mentawai Islands.

Most guests land at Padang's Minangkabau airport in the early afternoon; the crew meets you and drives you to the harbour, about an hour away. Unpack, rack the quiver and meet the surf guide for a briefing on the swell forecast, reef safety and the plan for the week. Dinner on deck as the boat clears the harbour, then the overnight run across the Mentawai Strait. Wake up to reef passes.

DAY 02 / 08AXS·01 · D02

The Playgrounds

Wake among the small reef islands south of Siberut, where lefts and rights sit a short tender ride apart. Dawn and afternoon sessions on the tide, then a dusk move to south Siberut.

The Playgrounds are a cluster of reef islands and passes where a handful of breaks face different ways, so something usually works whatever the wind. Your guide picks the first session from the deck at first light; tenders shuttle you to the line-up and back for breakfast. Midday is for sleeping, snorkelling the lagoon or fishing. The afternoon session follows the tide, then the boat moves a few miles to south Siberut for the night.

DAY 03 / 08AXS·01 · D03

South Siberut

A long morning on the outer reefs of south Siberut while the wind stays offshore, then the run south across the channel to north Sipora.

South Siberut faces the open Indian Ocean and picks up the long-period swells that travel thousands of kilometres from the Southern Ocean. The guide times a long morning session for the cleanest hours, often the outer reef left [break name TBC]. After lunch the boat crosses the channel, about 35 nautical miles, to the north end of Sipora, which offers sheltered options when the swell is big.

DAY 04 / 08AXS·01 · D04

North Sipora

Sheltered reefs in the morning, then down the west coast of Sipora to Katiet for an afternoon on the right-hand reef.

North Sipora has corners that hold shape when the swell is too big elsewhere, so it is the first choice on a heavy day. After the morning session the boat runs about 32 nautical miles down the coast to Katiet at the southern tip, where a right-hand reef breaks along a shallow shelf [break name TBC]. Stay for the evening glass-off.

DAY 05 / 08AXS·01 · D05

South Sipora

Dawn on the Katiet reef, then south across the strait to the west coast of Pagai Utara, the swell magnet of the chain.

A dawn session at Katiet, then a visit ashore to the village above the reef, home to a Mentawai community that has watched surfers arrive for three decades. Mid-morning the boat crosses about 20 nautical miles to Pagai Utara, whose west-facing reefs catch more swell than anywhere else on the route. Surf until dark.

DAY 06 / 08AXS·01 · D06

Pagai Utara

A full morning on the Pagai reefs, the longest session of the trip, then back north to Sipora for the evening.

If the forecast lines up, this is the day of the trip: long-period swell wrapping onto the west-facing reefs of Pagai Utara with the wind offshore through the morning. Surf until the tide turns, with lunch delivered by tender. In the afternoon the boat heads back north to Katiet, with a line out for dogtooth and wahoo on the way.

Travel information

Before you go
Getting there

Fly to Padang's Minangkabau International Airport (PDG), usually via Jakarta (CGK) or Kuala Lumpur (KUL). Arrive by early afternoon on day one; the crew meets you at the airport. On day eight, book flights leaving after 12:00.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities can buy an Indonesian visa on arrival or apply online for an e-VOA before travel. Passports need at least six months' validity and blank pages. Check the current rules for your nationality before booking.

Boards & gear

Bring a quiver of two to four boards, including a step-up for bigger swells, plus spare fins, leashes, reef booties and a helmet if you use one. Check board bag rules and fees with every airline on your route.

Surf level & safety

Most Mentawai breaks are over shallow reef. You should be comfortable duck-diving, reading sets and paddling out through currents. The guide briefs every break, and the crew carries a reef-cut and first-aid kit; serious injuries mean evacuation to Padang.

Health

Speak to a travel clinic about vaccinations and malaria prevention for the Mentawai Islands. Travel insurance must include surfing and medical evacuation.

Weather & swell

Warm year-round, around 26–31 °C, with water around 28–29 °C; board shorts or a thin top are enough. Swell, wind and tide decide each day, and the plan changes with the forecast.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
Where are the Mentawai Islands?

The Mentawai Islands are a chain off the west coast of Sumatra, Indonesia, about 90 nautical miles from Padang across the Mentawai Strait. The main islands are Siberut, Sipora, Pagai Utara and Pagai Selatan.

When is the best time to surf the Mentawais?

The main season is about April to October, when long-period swells from the Southern Ocean are most consistent and morning winds are often offshore. The shoulder months are less crowded with smaller swells.

What surf level do I need?

Most breaks suit intermediate to advanced surfers comfortable over shallow reef. The guide chooses breaks to match the group, and there are easier waves on smaller days.

How do I get to the boat?

Fly to Padang's Minangkabau International Airport (PDG), usually via Jakarta or Kuala Lumpur. The crew transfers you to the harbour and the boat crosses to the islands overnight.

How many surfers are on the boat?

The trip is a private charter for up to ten guests, so line-ups stay small. Partners who do not surf are welcome.

Is there a surf tax?

Visitors to the Mentawai Islands pay a regional surf tax. The amount is set locally and is confirmed before departure.
