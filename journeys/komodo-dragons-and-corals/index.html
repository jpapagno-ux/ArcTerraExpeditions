At a glance

The short answers

What is Dragons & Corals?

A six-day, five-night private voyage through Komodo National Park in Indonesia aboard the phinisi Dunia Baru, round trip from Labuan Bajo on Flores.

Who is it for?

Divers, snorkellers, families and wildlife lovers who want Komodo dragons, coral reefs and quiet anchorages away from the day boats.

What are the highlights?

The ranger walk for Komodo dragons on Rinca, the Padar viewpoint, the reefs of Gili Lawa, a full day at remote Banta, manta rays at Taka Makassar and flying foxes at Kalong.

How active is it?

Easy to moderate. One steep optional climb at Padar, a gentle ranger walk, and up to three dives or snorkels a day. Every activity is optional.

DAY 01 / 06AX·011 · D01

Labuan Bajo — Sabolan

Board Dunia Baru in Labuan Bajo, snorkel or check-dive the reefs of Sabolan, then anchor off Kalong as thousands of fruit bats rise from the mangroves at dusk.

Your journey begins in Labuan Bajo, the harbour town on the western tip of Flores. The crew welcomes you aboard Dunia Baru, and after a safety briefing we sail out among the islands to Sabolan. Spend the afternoon on its reefs, with a relaxed snorkel from the tender or a check dive with the dive team. As the light fades we anchor off Kalong, where thousands of flying foxes lift out of the mangroves and stream across the sky towards Flores. Sundowners on deck, then dinner at anchor.

DAY 02 / 06AX·011 · D02

Rinca — Padar

A morning ranger walk on Rinca to find Komodo dragons, water buffalo and macaques. Afternoon at Padar: dive, swim from a pink-sand beach or climb to the viewpoint over its three bays.

Early morning is the best time to walk on Rinca, before the heat. With a park ranger we follow the trails from the Loh Buaya ranger station in search of Komodo dragons, the largest lizards on earth, along with water buffalo, Timor deer, wild boar and long-tailed macaques. Back aboard for breakfast, we sail to Padar. Dive or snorkel its reefs, swim from one of its beaches, and in the late afternoon climb the ridge path to the famous viewpoint, where three curved bays of white, pink and black sand open beneath you. We stay at anchor below the island for the night.

DAY 03 / 06AX·011 · D03

Gili Lawa — Batu Monco

A reef morning around Gili Lawa with coral gardens, schooling fish and a chance of pelagics, then a cruise to the quiet bay of Batu Monco to kayak and snorkel.

We wake at Gili Lawa on the northern edge of the park, where currents from the Flores Sea feed some of the richest reefs in Komodo. Divers drop onto coral gardens and pinnacles busy with schooling fusiliers, reef sharks and, on the right tide, larger pelagics; snorkellers drift the shallows with the tender close by. After lunch we cruise west along the north coast of Komodo Island to Batu Monco, a quiet bay with very few other boats. Kayak along the shore, snorkel the fringing reef and watch the hills turn gold in the late light.

DAY 04 / 06AX·011 · D04

Banta

A full day at remote Banta Island: volcanic shoreline, healthy reefs and very few other boats, with paddleboards, kayaks and the wakeboard between dives.

Today we stay at Banta, a remote volcanic island between Komodo and Sumbawa that most boats never reach. Its reefs are healthy and quiet, with walls and slopes for divers and clear shallows for snorkellers. Between dives the water toys come out: paddleboards, kayaks, the wakeboard and the slide off the deck. There is time to do as much or as little as you like, with lunch on board and the island almost to yourselves.

DAY 05 / 06AX·011 · D05

Taka Makassar — Papangga

Manta rays ride the currents off the Taka Makassar sandbank. Afternoon swimming at Papangga, then a beach barbecue with your feet in the sand.

We sail back south-east to Taka Makassar, a sandbank that appears at low tide in the middle of the park. The channels nearby are known for manta rays feeding and cleaning in the current, and the dive team will plan the morning around the tide for the best chance of seeing them. After lunch we move to Papangga for an easy afternoon of swimming and snorkelling. As the sun sets, the crew lays a table on the beach for a barbecue under the stars, the last night of the voyage.

Travel information

Before you go
Getting there

Fly to Komodo Airport (LBJ) in Labuan Bajo, usually via Bali (DPS) or Jakarta (CGK). Arrive the day before or on a morning flight on day one; the crew meets you at the airport. On the last day, book flights leaving after midday.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities can buy an Indonesian visa on arrival or apply online for an e-VOA before travel. Passports need at least six months' validity and blank pages. Check the current rules for your nationality before you book.

Diving & snorkelling

Bring your certification card and logbook. Komodo is known for strong currents at some sites, such as Gili Lawa and the manta channels; the dive team matches sites to your experience, and every site has a snorkelling option from the tender.

Komodo dragons & safety

Walks on Rinca are always led by park rangers. Keep to the group, stay quiet and follow the ranger's instructions at all times. Guests who are menstruating or have open wounds should tell the ranger before the walk.

Health

Speak to a travel clinic about vaccinations for Flores. Labuan Bajo has basic medical facilities; travel insurance must include medical evacuation and dive cover.

Weather & sea

Hot and dry for most of the year, around 27–33 °C on land, with water around 26–29 °C. The south of the park can be cooler and choppier in July and August.

What to pack

Light clothes, a hat and sunglasses, closed shoes for Rinca and Padar, reef-safe sunscreen, a light rash guard or shorty wetsuit, a dry bag and any personal dive kit. Soft bags store best on board.

Questions travellers ask

FAQ
Where is Komodo National Park?

Komodo National Park lies between the Indonesian islands of Sumbawa and Flores, in East Nusa Tenggara. Voyages start and finish in Labuan Bajo, on the western tip of Flores, about an hour's flight east of Bali.

How do I get to Labuan Bajo?

Fly to Komodo Airport (LBJ) in Labuan Bajo. Most guests connect through Bali (DPS), with several short flights a day; some fly via Jakarta. The crew meets you at the airport on day one.

Will I see Komodo dragons?

Very likely. The ranger walk on Rinca on day two is one of the most reliable places to see Komodo dragons in the wild, but they are wild animals and sightings are never guaranteed. A park ranger leads every walk.

Do I need to scuba dive?

No. Every dive site on this route can be snorkelled from the tender, and the land days need no diving at all. Certified divers can dive up to three times a day with the on-board dive team.

When can I see manta rays?

Mantas are seen around Taka Makassar and the nearby channels for much of the year, most often during the rainy season from roughly December to April. The crew plans the dive around the tide for the best chance.

How hard is the Padar viewpoint climb?

The path is a steep stairway and track of about 30 to 40 minutes up, with no shade. It is optional and timed for the cooler late afternoon or early morning.

When is the best time to visit Komodo?

The dry season from about April to November brings calm seas and good visibility. The rainy months bring greener hills and more mantas. Exact Dunia Baru departure dates are to be confirmed.
